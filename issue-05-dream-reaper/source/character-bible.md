# Character And Reference Bible

## Shared Visual Style

Realistic candid photo-comic pages. The images should feel like documentary
phone photos or handheld photojournalism arranged into comic panels, even when
the subject is cosmic and dreamlike. Use believable camera perspective,
physical textures, natural imperfect framing, photoreal VFX, and clean
rectangular caption boxes. No anime, no painted illustration, no cartoon
rendering, no comic-book linework, no studio-poster polish except on the cover.

All readable story text belongs inside generated rectangular caption boxes or
cover title treatment inside the image art. No later text overlays.

## Planet-Man

- Role: protagonist, creator god, protector of Shrink-Scope's world, now
  haunted by the cost of destroying Madame Multiverse's dress.
- Body: divine humanoid with black rocky volcanic skin, rough stone texture,
  subtle molten fissures, and glowing molten lava eyes.
- Hair: green vine dreadlocks, organic and root-like, hanging around his face
  and shoulders.
- Outfit: fitted beaded bodysuit; every bead is a distinct tiny miniature
  planet with oceans, clouds, rock, ice, fire, rings, forests, metal, or alien
  terrain.
- Powers: Mini-Planetkinesis. He summons and reshapes miniature planets as
  shields, blades, cages, lightning rods, and lucid-dream constructs.
- Dream-state motif: cosmic sparkling purple light when he becomes lucid, with
  dream particles reflecting in his molten eyes.
- Emotional range: sleeping vulnerability, wonder, confusion, horror, pride,
  lucid focus, then panic on waking.

## Shrink-Scope

- Role: supporting character and emotional anchor in the real-world final
  scene.
- Body: heroic woman with polished wooden body and black ruler-like hash marks
  across skin and limbs.
- Face: concerned face with glowing white crystal-ball eyes.
- Hair: warm medium-brown hair pulled back in a practical tied-back style.
- Outfit: black crop top with a crystal microscope insignia, black miniskirt
  covered in silver stars, glittery black heels, practical HQ sleep-interrupt
  posture rather than battle posing.
- Personality: disciplined, protective, direct, deeply concerned when
  Planet-Man wakes screaming.
- Motif: crystal optics, microscope shapes, ruler marks, silver stars, black
  practical superhero gear.

## The Grim Reaper

- Role: antagonist inside the dream, possible supernatural accuser, and dark
  mirror of Planet-Man.
- Body and face: completely obscured by costume until the reveal. Inside the
  hood is pitch-black darkness with no skull, no eyes, and no visible face.
- Outfit: black hooded robe, very long hanging sleeves, black gloves, long robe
  that entirely hides the feet, ragged edges moving like smoke.
- Weapon: enormous curved scythe with a worn black handle and cold silver
  blade, capable of cutting miniature planets in half.
- Powers: dark magic shockwaves, shadow nightmare monsters, giant-size
  transformation, black lightning storm, oppressive dream control.
- Reveal continuity: when his hood falls on page 7, the face underneath is
  Planet-Man's own black rocky volcanic face with green vine dreadlocks and
  molten lava eyes.
- Silhouette: tall, faceless, footless, sleeves too long, scythe always
  readable, more like a physical robed figure captured in real footage than a
  cartoon skeleton.

## Nightmare Monsters

- Living shadow beasts with wet black smoke bodies, clawed hands, fanged jaws,
  and uneven silhouettes. They should feel like real volumetric darkness
  captured on camera, not drawn creatures.

## Key Locations

- Planet-Man HQ Bedroom: human-scale bedroom/workshop inside Shrink-Scope's
  hidden HQ, with a real bed, shelves of unfinished miniature worlds, black
  volcanic stone textures, green vine-like cables, molten orange accent lights,
  sketchbooks with no readable writing, and safe containment rings for
  boulder-sized planet projects.
- Dream Realm: colorful, ethereal, glowing space with misty depth, luminous
  auroras, floating exotic miniature planets, impossible scale, and soft
  photoreal cosmic haze. It should still be arranged as candid photo-comic
  panels rather than flat fantasy illustration.
