Issue 4 introduces Madame Multiverse as the villain.

Madame Multiverse has long weightless hair like the silver tail of a comet,
skin like colorful nebula dust, radiant blue starburst eyes, a frail skinny
ethereal alien frame, and a patchwork dress stitched from miniature universes.
Her dress is literally spacetime fabric. She creates and telekinetically
manipulates miniature universes.

She believes gods owe nothing to their creations and that gods should focus
only on other gods. She sees Planet-Man as the only worthy suitor on her world.
She is jealous that Planet-Man gives his attention to Shrink-Scope, whom she
sees as a pathetic mortal rival. Her goal is to convince Planet-Man to stop
saving a world that owes him nothing and to devote himself to her. If he will
not comply, she is willing to kill Shrink-Scope.

The issue opens with Shrink-Scope seeing a clairvoyant vision of Madame
Multiverse causing chaos in the streets by wrapping civilians inside miniature
toroidal universes and lifting them toward space. Planet-Man flies over the
city, pulls civilians out of the toroidal universes, and carries them back to
safety. Madame Multiverse reveals that the attack was bait to draw him out.

Planet-Man asks what she wants. Madame Multiverse tells him she has lived a
long lonely life as a god among mortals. Planet-Man admits he once felt lonely
as a god and created miniature planets to fill the void. She says she creates
miniature universes for the same reason, and now neither of them has to be
alone. Planet-Man explains that Shrink-Scope helped him find community and
purpose, and offers Madame Multiverse a chance to give up villainy and help the
world instead.

Madame Multiverse is disgusted. She argues that creations owe gods nothing and
that mortals are a waste of divine attention. She invites Planet-Man to leave
Shrink-Scope behind and spend eternity creating together. Planet-Man refuses,
saying he is tired of abandoning his creations. He credits Shrink-Scope with
helping him become who he is, and swears he will never betray her.

Madame Multiverse grins and threatens to kill Shrink-Scope if she must.
Planet-Man charges at her in rage, but she blocks him with a floating miniature
flat universe. The flat universe spins like a saw blade. She throws it at him
like a ninja star. Planet-Man counters with a spinning miniature ringed planet,
but the flat universe cleaves through the ring and cuts his left cheek as he
barely dodges.

Madame Multiverse attacks with a barrage of punches. Planet-Man is battered and
bruised, then summons orbiting miniature ice planets to block her. Her fists
shatter the ice planets easily. She taunts him that Shrink-Scope will not stand
a chance if he cannot beat her, and says a god of planets can never beat a god
of universes.

Planet-Man is weakened but confident. He says he will never let anyone hurt
Shrink-Scope. He would rather not kill Madame Multiverse, but sees no other
choice. He points out that every miniature universe she creates is populated by
countless miniature planets, and that her dress is an entire miniature
multiverse. Madame Multiverse realizes what he is about to do. Planet-Man
screams "Biggest Bang Bomb" as every miniature planet in every miniature
universe on her dress explodes at once. The blast launches Planet-Man backward
and seemingly atomizes Madame Multiverse.

Planet-Man returns to HQ and hugs Shrink-Scope tightly, promising to keep her
safe. Exhausted, he is helped to his room by Shrink-Scope, who assures him she
knows and has already prepared what he needs to recover from the battle.
