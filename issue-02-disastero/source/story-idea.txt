Planet-Man issue 2 introduces Disastero, an existential super-villain known as a walking apocalypse. Disastero has a head made of water, hair like a tidal wave, spinning tornado arms, a cracked gray stone torso, and lava legs. He can create and manipulate natural disasters with enough power to end the world.

Disastero lives in the Apocalypse Church, a massive castle where world leaders regularly bring offerings so he will spare their countries. Everyone who has challenged him has lost homes and loved ones to terrible disasters. Shrink-Scope has never tried to shrink him because she fears his world-ending power might remain potent even at miniature scale. Disastero is considered untouchable, unprisonable, and impossible to negotiate with.

Planet-Man arrives at the Apocalypse Church and tries to speak with him first. He appeals to the loneliness of godhood, wondering if Disastero destroys because he cannot create. Disastero rejects the comparison, mocks Planet-Man for having no church, offerings, or followers, and insists that might makes right. Planet-Man realizes Disastero is not lonely or misunderstood, only devoted to power.

Planet-Man laments that he will have to kill Disastero. Disastero dares him to try. Disastero attacks with lava, tornado force, and a power that destabilizes the cores of Planet-Man's mini-planets. Planet-Man survives bruised and burned, then hurls a boulder-sized mini-planet. Disastero ruptures its core before impact, but the planet was built from many smaller mini-planetoids under Planet-Man's control. The shattered pieces become a barrage of telekinetic bullets: Terra Trojan Buckshot.

Disastero is defeated. Shrink-Scope is impressed that Planet-Man did not let himself get distracted, but Planet-Man feels disappointed rather than victorious because this was not a redeemed god or a solved loneliness. It was the removal of an existential threat.
