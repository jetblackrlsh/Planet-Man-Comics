# Treatment

## Logline

Planet-Man confronts Disastero, a natural-disaster god-tyrant worshipped by terrified nations, and learns that some threats cannot be understood into mercy.

## Central Conflict

Planet-Man wants to believe another godlike being might be reached through shared loneliness. Disastero believes power is the only proof of divinity and treats worship, offerings, and fear as the natural order.

## Theme

Creation is not weak because it lacks worship. Power without conscience is not godhood; it is apocalypse wearing a crown.

## Ending

Planet-Man defeats Disastero with Terra Trojan Buckshot, a mini-planet secretly built from smaller controlled planetoids. The victory removes a world-ending threat, but Planet-Man is left unsettled by the need to kill rather than save.

## Emotional Arc

Planet-Man begins hopeful and reflective, projecting his own loneliness onto Disastero. The conversation strips that hope away. Disastero's attacks make the danger physical, but the real reversal is moral: Planet-Man accepts that focused creation may have to become lethal protection. He ends alive and successful, but disappointed by what the mission required.

## Cast

- Planet-Man: creator god and field hero, still learning discipline.
- Shrink-Scope: mission controller, pragmatic hero, afraid to shrink Disastero because the risk is too large.
- Disastero: walking apocalypse, tyrant of the Apocalypse Church, believer in divine right through catastrophic power.
- World leaders: silent supplicants bringing offerings to Disastero.
