# Planet-Man Series Idea

## Series Art Style

The series uses a realistic candid photo style. Characters, settings, powers, costumes, props, and cosmic elements should look as though they were captured by a real camera in an actual physical environment rather than drawn, painted, or staged as comic-book illustration. The photography should feel natural, immediate, and documentary-like, with believable lighting, real materials, grounded textures, imperfect framing, and lived-in environments.

Even when the subject matter is impossible, such as miniature planets, rocky divine skin, living wooden bodies, molten eyes, telekinetic flight, and shrunken villains, the visual treatment should make those elements feel physically present in the world. Avoid cartoon rendering, exaggerated superhero gloss, airbrushed fantasy art, and obvious studio-poster composition. The ideal look is candid, strange, and convincing.

## Planet-Man

Planet-Man is a god with the power of Mini-Planetkinesis. This ability allows him to psychically summon, control, and manipulate miniature planets. He is an artist at heart who enjoys the thrill of creating new worlds.

Planet-Man has a very short attention span and is easily distracted. Just as soon as he makes one world, he quickly gets an idea for another one and starts creating that instead. He has tons of worlds that he has long forgotten about, and he makes more and more every day.

The worlds he creates can be as large as a boulder or as small as a marble. He can freely move them around telekinetically, and he can even influence the basic idea of what each world is supposed to be like on the surface. Once a world is created, it evolves from the basic idea he had in a way that he can no longer control.

Planet-Man has green vine dreadlocks, black rocky skin, and molten lava eyes. He wears a beaded bodysuit, where every bead is actually a miniature planet. Thanks to this bodysuit, he can fly by using his powers to lift the bodysuit while he is wearing it.

## The Emotional Depth Behind Planet-Man's Artistic Pursuits and Distracted Personality

Planet-Man finds fulfillment in the act of creating new things, but the way his mind works does not allow him to enjoy them for very long. He does not hate his creations, but he has an obsessive drive to pursue novelty.

Things around him quickly become boring to him, and that feeling of boredom is insufferable. He creates new worlds to escape the boredom of mundane existence and to bring to life worlds that actually interest him.

The best way to think about how his mind works is that everything is on a timer. Once that timer is up, he is ready to cast it to the side and focus on something else that feels new and exciting.

He also prides himself on being creative and thinking outside of the box when creating worlds. The more exotic and unusual a world is, the better.

It becomes challenging to keep coming up with new world ideas after he has made so many, and that challenge gives him something to continue to strive for. It works well with his mind's never-ending chase for novelty.

## Planet-Man's Combat Ability

Planet-Man's fighting style is called Planet-Fu.

Planet-Fu focuses on creating miniature lifeless planets and using them as telekinetically manipulated drones. He can use these mini-planets to distract opponents, bludgeon opponents by moving around whatever barrier is protecting them and striking from another angle, or form constructs by coordinating multiple planets to move in sync with one another. These constructs can become particular moving shapes or structures made up of individual mini-planets.

He can create mini-planets with unstable cores that explode on impact like bombs. He can also create volcanic planets that are essentially balls of fire, ice planets covered in thick layers of frozen ice, gaseous planets that can have chemical effects when inhaled, ringed planets with sharp bladed rings, water planets, and a wide range of other specialized planet types.

Essentially, any kind of planet someone can think of, Planet-Man can make and telekinetically control as long as it is anywhere between the size of a marble and a boulder.

## Shrink-Scope

Shrink-Scope is a woman with clairvoyance and the power to change the size of one thing a day. Her planet was created by Planet-Man as a world with a large population of superpowered beings. However, Planet-Man did not specify the alignment of these beings, so Shrink-Scope is now one of the only superheroes on a world populated mostly by villains.

Because her main offensive power can only be used once a day, she cannot do much to protect her world on a large scale. She has created a miniature prison where she securely stores the villains she has shrunk down. But even if she shrank one villain a day, she would never make a significant dent in the world's villain population.

Shrink-Scope has a heroic spirit that is seemingly unmatched on her world. Where others with powers want to use their abilities for personal gain, she is purely motivated to purge her world of the forces of evil. It is almost as though the world itself evolved to ensure that she contained the purest of intentions.

She has glowing white crystal ball eyes. Her body is made of wood and is covered in black hash marks like a ruler. She wears a black crop top with a crystal microscope insignia, a black miniskirt covered in silver stars, and glittery black heels.

## Basic Idea of the Series

In an effort to save her world from the forces of evil, Shrink-Scope uses her incredible powers to shrink down Planet-Man so he can help her stop the villains plaguing her world. This does not make him tiny by the standards of Shrink-Scope's world. It shrinks him down to a normal usable size for her world, so he can move through its cities and fight its villains like a normal-sized person there.

Using her clairvoyance, Shrink-Scope can locate where villains are and dispatch Planet-Man to stop them. Planet-Man does not possess the power to restore himself to normal size, so he does not have much of a choice but to help Shrink-Scope save her world if he wants to be restored to his original size.

Due to Planet-Man's artistic spirit and easily distracted mind, Shrink-Scope has to keep an eye on him and help him focus on the task at hand whenever he is on a mission.

## How Justified Is Shrink-Scope in Shrinking Planet-Man

Shrink-Scope feels morally justified in shrinking Planet-Man to help her defend her world.

She was legitimately outnumbered on a world where most superpowered beings use their abilities for personal gain without moral consideration. She felt desperate for help and did not have another superpowered person to turn to.

The only superpowered being she was aware of who was not a villain was Planet-Man, and she was largely aware of Planet-Man thanks to her own clairvoyant abilities.

## What Shrink-Scope Thinks of Planet-Man

Shrink-Scope is glad that Planet-Man is not evil.

While it is annoying how easily distracted he is, she is used to being around superpowered individuals who are self-centered. Those who care only about themselves are quick to intentionally make others suffer for their own benefit.

Planet-Man is different because he is not obsessed with himself. He is obsessed with the act of creation. He did not create her world for it to suffer intentionally, and that matters to her.

She can work with that. In a world under threat by destructive supervillains, a true creator is exactly what her planet needs to survive.
