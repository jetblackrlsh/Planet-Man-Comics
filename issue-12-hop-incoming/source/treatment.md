# Treatment

## Logline

Hiding in a forest on a random miniature world, Hoppette briefly bonds with a runaway talking dog before Bruiser, a violent hunter from her own world, follows her hop signature and forces her to choose escape over rescue.

## Central Conflict

Hoppette wants to stay hidden long enough to survive and keep resisting her world's campaign against other gods. Bruiser wants to drag her home, using both religious accusation and the threat to an innocent dog to flush her out.

## Theme

Faith becomes blasphemy when it tries to erase another world's god. Survival can require restraint even when compassion demands action.

## Ending

Hoppette escapes through a lily pad glyph to the one place Bruiser will not follow: Planet-Man's world. Bruiser is left furious in the clearing, with the captured dog behind him and the open threat that Planet-Man has become part of Hoppette's refuge.

## Emotional Arc

- Hoppette begins tired but gentle, finding kinship with a stray dog who also ran away from home.
- The red "Hop Incoming" warning snaps her into trained fear, revealing that she has lived with pursuit long enough to react before thinking.
- Bruiser's attack on the dog tests Hoppette's instinct to protect the vulnerable against the strategic need to stay hidden.
- When she accidentally reveals herself, the chase turns the moral argument physical: treetops, tongue-swinging, laser fire, and competing claims about true blasphemy.
- Hoppette ends wounded by the choice to leave the dog, but clear enough to escape toward Planet-Man rather than be taken back.

## Cast

- **Hoppette:** Runaway inventor of the hop suits, now resisting her world's attempt to replace or displace the gods of other worlds.
- **The stray dog:** A talking dog from the random miniature forest world; curious, frightened, and innocent enough to expose Hoppette's compassion.
- **Bruiser:** A muscular humanoid frog man and hunter from Hoppette's world, loyal to its restored "true god" ideology and willing to hurt bystanders to recapture her.

## Visual Strategy

The issue should feel like realistic candid wildlife photography and handheld pursuit footage inside an impossible miniature-planet forest. The campfire gives warm amber intimacy at the start. Bruiser brings harsh red warning light, white glyph glow, wet mucus, and ruby cybernetic laser fire. The final clearing should contrast cool moonlit grass and hot laser scars, with Hoppette's lily pad glyph as the clean escape shape.
