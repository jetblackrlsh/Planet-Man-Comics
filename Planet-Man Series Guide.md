# Planet-Man Series Guide

This document tracks the issues in the Planet-Man comic series and summarizes
what each issue establishes for the ongoing story.

## Series Premise

Planet-Man is a creator god with Mini-Planetkinesis: the ability to create and
telekinetically control miniature planets. He is imaginative, restless, easily
distracted, and driven by the thrill of making new worlds. Shrink-Scope is a
hero from one of his forgotten worlds. She has clairvoyance and can change the
size of one thing per day.

Shrink-Scope's world is overrun by superpowered villains, so she shrinks
Planet-Man down to normal usable scale for her world and recruits him into a
mission structure: she locates the threat, sends Planet-Man into the field,
keeps him focused, and helps him use his creative powers to protect the world
he once left behind.

## Issue 1: Planet-Man: First Orbit

**Status:** Complete  
**Location:** `intro-issue/`  
**PDF:** `intro-issue/output/pdf/planet-man-first-orbit.pdf`

### Summary

Planet-Man is introduced as a creator god who can make miniature worlds but
quickly loses interest once a new idea appears. Every bead on his bodysuit is
another tiny planet, many of them forgotten after their first spark of novelty.

Shrink-Scope is introduced as the disciplined hero of one of those forgotten
worlds. Her planet is filled with superpowered people, but most of them use
their abilities selfishly or destructively. With clairvoyance and the power to
change the size of one thing per day, she has built a miniature prison for
captured villains, but she cannot save her world fast enough alone.

Using her clairvoyance, Shrink-Scope finds Planet-Man and uses her daily
size-change power to bring him down to normal scale within her world. Planet-Man
is startled and indignant, but Shrink-Scope frames the act as recruitment rather
than rescue. She needs the maker of her world to help protect it.

Their first mission pits them against Lord Radius, a city-level villain whose
expanding pressure fields threaten a damaged city block. The issue demonstrates
the structure of future Planet-Man stories: Shrink-Scope sees the danger,
Planet-Man enters the field, his attention wanders, the stakes rise, and
Shrink-Scope has to refocus him before people suffer.

Planet-Man uses Planet-Fu, turning miniature worlds into tactical tools: a
volcanic planet as a warning shot, an ice world as a protective wall, a ringed
planet to cut a force field, and a gas giant to disrupt the villain. The mission
becomes more than spectacle when Planet-Man sees civilians and damaged homes as
evidence that his world kept living after he looked away.

In the end, Planet-Man chooses responsibility over novelty. Lord Radius is
captured and stored in Shrink-Scope's miniature prison. The city block survives.
Planet-Man still wants to make impossible new worlds, but now his ideas are tied
to a mission: find the threat, focus the maker, save the world.

### Characters Established

- **Planet-Man:** Creator god, artist, novelty seeker, easily distracted hero in
  progress. Powers include Mini-Planetkinesis, flight through his planet-bead
  bodysuit, and Planet-Fu.
- **Shrink-Scope:** Focused mission controller and field hero. Powers include
  clairvoyance and one size-change per day. She is morally serious, strategic,
  and protective of her world.
- **Lord Radius:** First mission villain. His pressure-ring powers demonstrate
  the kind of city-level threat Planet-Man and Shrink-Scope will face.

### Series Elements Established

- Planet-Man created Shrink-Scope's world but did not guide its evolution after
  the initial idea.
- Shrink-Scope's world has many superpowered villains and very few heroes.
- Shrink-Scope's HQ includes mission control, living quarters, miniature prison
  storage, and world-storage space for Planet-Man's creations.
- Shrink-Scope's miniature prison is a recurring containment tool.
- Planet-Fu is Planet-Man's combat style, using specialized miniature planets as
  drones, shields, weapons, traps, and constructs.
- The core story engine is: Shrink-Scope locates a threat, Planet-Man is sent to
  confront it, distraction creates risk, focus turns creation into protection,
  and the mission ends with a saved part of the world.

### Ongoing Threads

- Planet-Man must learn what responsibility means for a creator whose creations
  keep living after he moves on.
- Shrink-Scope must balance desperation, moral judgment, and the cost of
  recruiting Planet-Man by force.
- The villain population of Shrink-Scope's world remains too large for one hero
  to handle alone.
- Future issues can explore different villain powers, different specialized
  mini-planets, and the tension between Planet-Man's creative impulses and the
  discipline required to save a world.

## Issue 2: Planet-Man: Disastero

**Status:** Complete  
**Location:** `issue-02-disastero/`  
**PDF:** `issue-02-disastero/output/pdf/planet-man-disastero.pdf`

### Summary

Disastero is introduced as a walking apocalypse whose elemental body embodies
his power: water head, tidal-wave hair, tornado arms, cracked stone torso, and
lava legs. His natural-disaster control is so vast that world leaders regularly
bring offerings to the Apocalypse Church so he will spare their countries.

Shrink-Scope explains that she has never tried to shrink Disastero because even
a miniature version might retain enough power to end everything. Planet-Man
chooses to speak with Disastero first, hoping another godlike being might
understand the loneliness of godhood.

The conversation fails. Disastero mocks Planet-Man for having no church,
offerings, or followers, rejecting creation as weak and treating worship as the
proof of divine right. Planet-Man realizes Disastero is not lonely or
misunderstood; he only believes that might makes right.

Disastero overwhelms Planet-Man with lava, tornado force, and the ability to
destabilize the cores of Planet-Man's suit-worlds. Injured and burned,
Planet-Man makes one final attack with a boulder-sized mini-planet. Disastero
ruptures it before impact, but Planet-Man had built that world from many
smaller planetoids under his control. The fragments become Terra Trojan
Buckshot, defeating Disastero.

Shrink-Scope is impressed that Planet-Man stayed focused, but Planet-Man feels
more disappointed than victorious. Disastero is gone, and the world is safer,
but the mission leaves Planet-Man with the cost of removing a godlike threat
rather than redeeming one.

### Characters Established

- **Disastero:** Existential super-villain, disaster tyrant, and ruler of the
  Apocalypse Church. His power level makes prison or shrinking too risky.

### Series Elements Established

- Some villains in Shrink-Scope's world cannot safely be contained by the
  miniature prison.
- Planet-Man can design mini-planets with hidden structures and use their
  fragments as independent telekinetic weapons.
- Planet-Man's empathy is valuable, but it can become a liability when a threat
  only understands power.

## Issue 3: Planet-Man: Scalard

**Status:** Complete  
**Location:** `issue-03-scalard/`  
**PDF:** `issue-03-scalard/output/pdf/planet-man-scalard.pdf`

### Summary

Scalard is introduced as an asymmetrical wizard who sees evil as the true
natural order. His split form embodies his philosophy: one face half is red
with a black devil horn, the other shimmering white with half of a gold halo,
and his black-and-white robe turns moral contrast into ritual costume.

Shrink-Scope's clairvoyance fails when Scalard creates a magical smokescreen
that blocks her crystal sight. She can still feel the source and sends
Planet-Man to investigate, leading him into Scalard's Chamber of Uneven Scales.

Scalard argues that balance is an illusion and that a world dominated by
villains makes heroism the violation. Planet-Man pushes back by explaining that
this world was not created with a fixed moral alignment, which means it can
still change in either direction.

Scalard reveals that the smokescreen was bait. His magic lets him recognize
that Planet-Man is truly the world's creator and also that Planet-Man does not
belong at its scale. Scalard offers a genuine consent-based spell that would
restore Planet-Man to his original size and let him leave, but only if he stops
helping Shrink-Scope.

Planet-Man is tempted because the offer is real, but he remembers the lonely
life he had before: worlds created in bursts of novelty and then left to gather
dust. He refuses to abandon this world again.

Scalard attacks with polymorph magic. Planet-Man blocks the beams with orbiting
mini-planets, losing several as they transform into a frog, rabbit, and
squirrel. When Scalard knocks him down and prepares a final spell, Planet-Man
summons a reflective mini ice planet. The bolt bounces back and transforms
Scalard into a harmless mouse.

Back at HQ, Shrink-Scope's clairvoyance is clear again. She notices that
Planet-Man's focus has improved, and Planet-Man tells her it is because he has
finally found something worth focusing on.

### Characters Established

- **Scalard:** Philosophical wizard-villain who believes evil is the true
  natural order and uses magic to keep the world's scales tilted toward
  villainy.

### Series Elements Established

- Some magic can recognize Planet-Man's true godhood and his mismatch with the
  scale of Shrink-Scope's world.
- Planet-Man could potentially leave the world if he willingly accepts the
  right spell, making his continued help an active choice.
- The world was not created with a fixed moral alignment, so its future can be
  shaped by choices rather than destiny.
- Planet-Man's improved focus comes from choosing responsibility over escape.

## Issue 4: Planet-Man: Madame Multiverse

**Status:** Complete  
**Location:** `issue-04-madame-multiverse/`  
**PDF:** `issue-04-madame-multiverse/output/pdf/planet-man-madame-multiverse.pdf`

### Summary

Madame Multiverse is introduced as a lonely cosmic god with silver comet-tail
hair, nebula-dust skin, radiant blue starburst eyes, and a patchwork dress made
from stitched miniature universes. Her power to create and manipulate tiny
universes lets her trap civilians inside toroidal cosmoses and lift them toward
space.

Shrink-Scope sees the attack in a clairvoyant vision and sends Planet-Man into
the city. Planet-Man rescues the civilians, but Madame Multiverse reveals that
the attack was only meant to draw his attention. She recognizes him as another
lonely god and offers him a life of endless creation together.

Planet-Man admits that he once made planets to fill the void, but tells Madame
Multiverse that Shrink-Scope gave him community and purpose. Madame rejects the
idea that gods owe care to mortals, demands that he leave Shrink-Scope behind,
and threatens to kill Shrink-Scope if he refuses.

The battle escalates quickly. Madame blocks Planet-Man's enraged punch with a
flat universe, spins it into a blade, cuts through his ringed mini-planet, and
wounds his cheek. She then batters him with star-like punches and shatters his
ice-world defenses, insisting that a god of planets cannot beat a god of
universes.

Planet-Man realizes that every universe Madame creates contains countless
planets, and that her dress is an entire miniature multiverse. He uses Biggest
Bang Bomb to detonate the tiny planets inside her dress all at once, seemingly
atomizing Madame Multiverse and blasting himself backward.

Back at HQ, Planet-Man hugs Shrink-Scope and promises to keep her safe.
Exhausted from the fight, he lets Shrink-Scope support him toward his room,
where she has already prepared what he needs to recover.

### Characters Established

- **Madame Multiverse:** Lonely cosmic god and jealous suitor who believes gods
  owe nothing to their creations. Her miniature-universe powers are greater in
  scale than Planet-Man's, but they still contain the planetary structures he
  can command.

### Series Elements Established

- Planet-Man's loyalty to Shrink-Scope is now explicit and personal, not only a
  mission obligation.
- Planet-Man's Mini-Planetkinesis can affect planets inside larger miniature
  cosmic structures, including universes created by another godlike being.
- Divine loneliness remains a temptation for Planet-Man, but he now defines
  purpose through relationship and responsibility rather than endless creation.

### Ongoing Threads

- Planet-Man is increasingly willing to use lethal force when Shrink-Scope is
  threatened.
- Shrink-Scope's role has evolved from recruiter and controller into the person
  who gives Planet-Man a reason to stay.
- Madame Multiverse is seemingly atomized, but her powers and multiverse nature
  leave room for cosmic consequences if the series revisits her.

## Issue 5: Planet-Man: Dream Reaper

**Status:** Complete  
**Location:** `issue-05-dream-reaper/`  
**PDF:** `issue-05-dream-reaper/output/pdf/planet-man-dream-reaper.pdf`

### Summary

After the destruction of Madame Multiverse's dress, Planet-Man falls asleep in
his HQ bedroom and dreams himself into a colorful, glowing realm of exotic
miniature planets. The beauty turns threatening when a faceless Grim Reaper
appears and slices one of the tiny worlds in half with his scythe.

The Reaper accuses Planet-Man of stealing his job. Planet-Man is confused, but
the Reaper explains that he earned the title god of Death by claiming more
lives than anyone else. By destroying the miniature multiverse inside Madame
Multiverse's dress, Planet-Man has broken that record. To reclaim the title,
the Reaper decides he must kill Planet-Man.

Planet-Man fights defensively at first, using ringed planets to parry the
scythe and boulder-sized planets to hold back nightmare monsters made of living
shadow. He insists that he did not know the dress contained living worlds and
that he does not want to be the God of Death. He declares himself a god of
creation, but the Reaper taunts him when Planet-Man destroys the monsters with
detonating planet cores.

The Reaper grows into a storm giant and rains black lightning across the dream.
Planet-Man creates metal miniature planets as lightning rods, then realizes
that the Reaper's impossible power comes from the battle taking place inside
his own dream. Once lucid, he reshapes the black lightning into flying soldiers
that carry the Reaper away.

As the Reaper is lifted into the dream distance, his hood falls back and
reveals Planet-Man's own face. Planet-Man wakes screaming in a cold sweat.
Shrink-Scope races in, hugs him, and tells him it was just a dream. Planet-Man
answers that it was real.

### Characters Established

- **The Grim Reaper:** A faceless black-robed dream antagonist who claims the
  title god of Death and treats Planet-Man's destruction of the miniature
  multiverse as a challenge to that title.

### Series Elements Established

- The deaths inside Madame Multiverse's dress now have explicit psychological
  and possibly supernatural consequences.
- Planet-Man can become lucid inside a dream and reshape dream phenomena using
  his creator-god identity.
- The Grim Reaper's final revealed face suggests that the threat may be a dark
  mirror of Planet-Man rather than only an outside enemy.

### Ongoing Threads

- Planet-Man's self-image as a god of creation is now in conflict with the
  death toll of his most powerful attacks.
- Shrink-Scope remains his emotional anchor, but she cannot simply dismiss what
  he experienced.
- The boundary between dream, guilt, and real cosmic consequence is unresolved.

## Issue 6: Planet-Man: Nothing Much

**Status:** Complete  
**Location:** `issue-06-shrink-scope-bank-heist/`  
**PDF:** `issue-06-shrink-scope-bank-heist/output/pdf/planet-man-nothing-much.pdf`

### Summary

While Planet-Man is away on another mission, Shrink-Scope has a clairvoyant
vision of a bank robbery. She decides not to call Planet-Man because the threat
appears small enough for her to handle alone, especially since she has already
used her one daily size alteration.

The robber first looks ordinary, but Shrink-Scope absorbs his full-speed impact
without moving, catches bullets in her compressed wooden arms, and uses
future-sight to dodge his super-strength attacks. She launches him through the
clouds with an uppercut, catches him when he falls unconscious, and hands him to
the police.

Back at HQ, Planet-Man asks what she has been doing. Shrink-Scope answers with
casual understatement: "Nothing much."

### Characters Established

- **Bank Robber:** A supposedly ordinary robber whose super strength turns a
  street-level crime into a real test of Shrink-Scope's solo capability.

### Series Elements Established

- Shrink-Scope is dangerous even when she cannot use her daily size change.
- Her wooden body, clairvoyance, and discipline make her more than a mission
  controller for Planet-Man.
- The series can support smaller, character-focused missions where importance is
  not measured by cosmic scale.

## Issue 7: Planet-Man: Everything Will Be Okay

**Status:** Complete  
**Location:** `issue-07-everything-will-be-okay/`  
**PDF:** `issue-07-everything-will-be-okay/output/pdf/planet-man-everything-will-be-okay.pdf`

### Summary

Shrink-Scope asks Planet-Man if he is happy and whether he resents being kept
on her world. Planet-Man reassures her that even though he did not choose to
arrive there at first, he has found purpose, community, and a real home.

Planet-Man then admits that his battle with Madame Multiverse still haunts him.
He fears that the planets inside her multiverse dress may have been inhabited
when he destroyed them. Shrink-Scope uses clairvoyance to look back at the
destroyed worlds and sees the truth: lives were lost.

To save Planet-Man from that guilt, Shrink-Scope lies and tells him the worlds
were empty. Planet-Man sleeps peacefully for the first time in a long while,
but Shrink-Scope cries alone in bed, repeating that everything will be okay
because she needs to believe it.

### Series Elements Established

- Planet-Man's stay in Shrink-Scope's world is now an emotional choice, not only
  the result of forced recruitment.
- Shrink-Scope is willing to carry a terrible truth alone to protect
  Planet-Man's fragile peace.
- The deaths inside Madame Multiverse's dress are confirmed as real within
  Shrink-Scope's clairvoyant view.

### Ongoing Threads

- Shrink-Scope's lie creates a private burden that may eventually threaten her
  trust with Planet-Man.
- Planet-Man's guilt is temporarily eased, not actually resolved.

## Issue 8: Planet-Man: Machine Monarch

**Status:** Complete  
**Location:** `issue-08-machine-monarch/`  
**PDF:** `issue-08-machine-monarch/output/pdf/planet-man-machine-monarch.pdf`

### Summary

Machine Monarch is already shrunken inside Shrink-Scope's miniature prison. His
cell blocks his machine-control power with a jammer, and its reinforced walls
prevent brute-force escape. He studies the routines of the prison instead.

When a charging pad flips out of the wall, Machine Monarch uses it as a two-way
interface. He pushes signals through the charging system, reaches his cell's
network, shuts down the jammer, and takes control of the robot guards.

Machine Monarch does not escape because he remains tiny, but he turns the
miniature prison into his first captured kingdom and becomes its unofficial
warden while plotting revenge.

### Characters Established

- **Machine Monarch:** A black robot tyrant with green circuit patterns, blue
  LED eyes, a circuit-board crown, and a wire cape. He thinks like
  infrastructure rather than a brawler.
- **Miniature Prison Robot Guards:** Security machines that can be subverted by
  Machine Monarch once his jammer is disabled.

### Series Elements Established

- Shrink-Scope's miniature prison is not only storage; it is a complex system
  with guards, charging routines, and hidden network vulnerabilities.
- Machine Monarch can control machines through indirect infrastructure access,
  making him a long-term threat even while shrunken.

## Issue 9: Planet-Man: Hoppette

**Status:** Complete  
**Location:** `issue-09-hoppette/`  
**PDF:** `issue-09-hoppette/output/pdf/planet-man-hoppette.pdf`

### Summary

Planet-Man and Shrink-Scope return to HQ exhausted, only for Hoppette to hop
out of a glowing white lily pad glyph on the floor. They treat her as an
intruder. Hoppette is too fast and agile for simple pursuit, but Shrink-Scope
uses clairvoyance to predict her landing point and Planet-Man traps her in the
orbit of a miniature gravity planet.

Hoppette explains that she is not an enemy. Her suit lets her travel between
miniature planets, and she has come to ask why Planet-Man helps only
Shrink-Scope's world when so many of his creations still need him.

Planet-Man says all worlds matter but he cannot be everywhere at once.
Shrink-Scope turns the dilemma into a practical idea: Planet-Man can create
Golem Guardians made from miniature planets to help worlds he cannot personally
visit. Hoppette leaves through a lily pad glyph carrying the first prototype.

### Characters Established

- **Hoppette:** A humanoid frog woman from another miniature world. She moves by
  superhuman hopping, sticks to walls with mucus, and travels between worlds
  through lily pad glyphs.
- **Miniature-Planet Golem / Golem Guardian:** A created helper body made from
  many miniature planets, built to extend Planet-Man's protection beyond one
  world.

### Series Elements Established

- Planet-Man's responsibility now extends explicitly to many neglected
  miniature worlds, not only Shrink-Scope's world.
- Hoppette's hop suit creates a new travel network between miniature planets.
- The Golem Guardian program begins as a compromise between care and scale.

## Issue 10: Planet-Man: Blizzard Redesignation

**Status:** Complete  
**Location:** `issue-10-blizzard-redesignation/`  
**PDF:** `issue-10-blizzard-redesignation/output/pdf/planet-man-blizzard-redesignation.pdf`

### Summary

Char Cole, a fire-powered supervillain, brings the defeated ice Golem Guardian
Blizzard to Doctor Metal Fist's lab. Blizzard is treated like a specimen while
the villains scan him to understand where the new guardians came from.

Doctor Metal Fist recognizes that the Golem Guardians were created by
Planet-Man and that their miniature worlds are connected by root systems that
function like muscles and nerves. He angrily insists that the guardians are
alive. When Char Cole takes offense at the doctor's insults, the villains begin
fighting each other.

Hoppette uses the distraction to hop into the lab, rescue Blizzard, and take him
to a different world. She explains that under her deal with Planet-Man and
Shrink-Scope, she helps them and they provide Golem Guardians to protect other
worlds. Blizzard is still weak, but he is redesignated to a world where his ice
body will not be so easily countered.

### Characters Established

- **Blizzard:** A living Golem Guardian made from miniature ice planets.
- **Char Cole:** A fire-bodied supervillain whose flames hard-counter Blizzard.
- **Doctor Metal Fist:** A cruel mad scientist with robotic arms who understands
  the biological implications of the Golem Guardians.

### Series Elements Established

- Golem Guardians are alive, not disposable tools.
- Hoppette is actively managing guardian deployment across worlds.
- The Golem Guardian program creates ethical questions about agency,
  assignment, and protection.

## Issue 11: Planet-Man: Lily Pad Lesson

**Status:** Complete  
**Location:** `issue-11-lily-pad-lesson/`  
**PDF:** `issue-11-lily-pad-lesson/output/pdf/planet-man-lily-pad-lesson.pdf`

### Summary

Hoppette invites Shrink-Scope to spar in a combat training room on another
miniature planet. Shrink-Scope begins cocky because she has beaten Hoppette
before, but Hoppette blinds Shrink-Scope's crystal eyes with mucus, blocks her
future sight, and speed-blitzes her until the adhesive buildup pins her in
place.

Once Shrink-Scope cannot ignore her, Hoppette explains the real reason for the
match. She is grateful that Shrink-Scope helped Planet-Man care about his
creations, but she is frustrated that Shrink-Scope's world still receives the
real Planet-Man while other worlds receive proxies.

Shrink-Scope argues that the Golem Guardian program exists for exactly that
problem. Hoppette counters that fixed guardians can be exploited by villains who
understand their limits. Hoppette frees Shrink-Scope and takes her home,
leaving Shrink-Scope without an easy answer.

### Series Elements Established

- Hoppette can defeat Shrink-Scope by targeting the physical limits of her
  clairvoyance.
- The Golem Guardian program is useful but imperfect because each guardian has a
  fixed toolset and exploitable weaknesses.
- Shrink-Scope's attachment to Planet-Man's presence is now part of the moral
  conflict over his divided responsibility.

## Issue 12: Planet-Man: Hop Incoming

**Status:** Complete  
**Location:** `issue-12-hop-incoming/`  
**PDF:** `issue-12-hop-incoming/output/pdf/planet-man-hop-incoming.pdf`

### Summary

Hoppette hides by a campfire on a random miniature planet and meets a talking
dog who also ran away from home. A red warning announces "Hop Incoming," and
Hoppette hides before Bruiser arrives through a lily pad glyph.

Bruiser attacks the dog with mucus to flush Hoppette out. When Hoppette
accidentally reveals herself, Bruiser chases her through the treetops. Their
argument reveals that Hoppette's people are trying to replace the gods of other
worlds and that she sees that mission as blasphemy now that Planet-Man has
returned.

Bruiser reveals a ruby cybernetic laser eye and nearly corners Hoppette. She
escapes through a lily pad glyph to the one world where he will not follow her:
Planet-Man's world. Bruiser vows that she cannot hide behind Planet-Man
forever.

### Characters Established

- **Bruiser:** A muscular frog-man hunter with a black eyepatch, cybernetic
  laser eye, tongue grapple, and loyalty to the Hoppers' true-god ideology.
- **The Hoppers:** Hoppette's people, who use hop suits and believe they can
  fill or replace divine roles on other worlds.
- **Talking Dog:** An innocent bystander whose danger exposes Hoppette's
  compassion and restraint.

### Series Elements Established

- Hoppette is a fugitive from her own world.
- The Hoppers consider Planet-Man "the abandoner" and view themselves as true
  gods.
- Bruiser's pursuit connects Hoppette's past directly to Planet-Man's HQ.

## Issue 13: Planet-Man: Shock Step

**Status:** Complete  
**Location:** `issue-13-shock-step/`  
**PDF:** `issue-13-shock-step/output/pdf/planet-man-shock-step.pdf`

### Summary

Planet-Man chases Shock Step, a blue-lightning speedster who has robbed a
jewelry store. Planet-Man is so exhausted that he loses consciousness mid-flight
and crashes onto a rooftop while Shock Step escapes taunting him.

Two Golem Guardians arrive: one rescues Planet-Man while the other continues
the chase. When Planet-Man wakes in bed, Shrink-Scope confronts him about
staying up too late. Planet-Man explains that the backlog of worlds feels
morally urgent and that he has been obsessively creating specialized guardians
for each one.

Shrink-Scope helps him identify the pattern: his old compulsive creation rush
has returned under the cover of responsibility. They agree to spend time
together each night before bed so he can stop, settle down, and rest.

### Characters Established

- **Shock Step:** A speedster supervillain made from blue lightning, with copper
  wire hair and battery-disc eyes.

### Series Elements Established

- Planet-Man's attempt to repair every abandoned world risks becoming another
  self-destructive obsession.
- Golem Guardians can operate as field responders independent of Planet-Man.
- Shrink-Scope now functions as a boundary-setter for Planet-Man's health, not
